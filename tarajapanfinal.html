    Japanese Government (MEXT) Scholarship, undergraduate

    • Applied for through the Japanese embassy in Manila, one call a year.
    • 2027 intake: open to high school graduates in good academic standing, under 25 by 1 April 2027.
    • Runs five years, with a stipend of ¥117,000 a month (2027 intake figure).
    • The 2027 call opened 22 April 2026 and closed 29 May 2026. The next call date is not confirmed yet, so watch the embassy page.

    Embassy of Japan in the Philippines: 2027 MEXT call

    Did you know? There is a second MEXT route: university recommendation

    Most people only hear about the embassy route above. MEXT also lets some Japanese universities nominate students themselves.

    • Embassy recommendation: candidates are recruited and first screened by a Japanese embassy. University recommendation: Japanese universities recruit candidates, based on student-exchange agreements with schools overseas. Only MEXT-approved universities can nominate, and not all can.
    • At Kyushu University, undergraduates have both tracks. The university picks a small number of recommended candidates from applicants who pass its entrance exam, and MEXT makes the final selection. Some schools use a Special Admission Track.
    • It is not the same everywhere. Some universities only nominate students from partner schools, and one university says its general university-recommendation frame is not planned for 2027 arrivals while the special program stays. Kyoto University says its general graduate version is expected to be discontinued from 2027. Check your target school's page.

    Questions

    Is TaraJapan free?

    The checklist, budget planner and scholarship list are free. Paid courses and review are planned, and prices will be shown before you sign up.

    Are you an agency?

    No. We are a student startup project that helps you understand the routes and prepare. We do not apply for you or promise admission.

    Do I need Japanese to study in Japan?

    Not always. Some degrees are taught in English, usually with an English test requirement. Learning basic Japanese still helps with daily life, and Japanese-taught degrees and many language schools need more.

    Do I need a visa?

    Yes. To study at a Japanese university you need a Student visa. You first need a Certificate of Eligibility (COE), which your school usually applies for. See the Visa tab for the steps and the official links, including the TB screening that applies to Philippine passport holders.

    How do I avoid scams?

    Never pay anyone who guarantees admission or a visa. Check schools on official sources such as Study in Japan (JASSO), and ask for costs in writing before you pay.

    Are you connected to JASSO, MEXT or a school?

    No. We link to their official pages, but we are independent.
